DECISION THRESHOLD CALIBRATION
==============================================================================
Active policy: minimise expected rupee loss subject to recall >= 90%,
pricing a missed fraud at its transaction amount and a false alarm at Rs.150.
Calibrated on 5-fold out-of-fold probabilities over train+validation
(~400 positives), never on the test set.

  Shipped threshold         : 0.2682
  Precision-at-recall point : 0.6987  (recall 0.9350, precision 0.9973 out-of-fold)
  Cost-optimal point        : 0.2682  (recall 0.9975, expected loss Rs.25,982)
  F1-optimal threshold      : 0.5399 (F1 = 0.9736)
  Single-split alternative  : 0.6766  (what the 80-positive validation split alone would have suggested)
  Default baseline          : 0.50

  Out-of-fold comparison (400 fraud rows, the largest sample available):
                                Precision   Recall       F1   Alarms  Missed   Expected loss
    default 0.50                   0.9701   0.9725   0.9713       12      11      Rs.550,243
    precision-at-recall 0.6987     0.9973   0.9350   0.9652        1      26    Rs.1,151,152
    cost-optimal 0.2682            0.8599   0.9975   0.9236       65       1       Rs.25,982

  Why the two policies disagree, and why 'cost' is the one shipping:
  the 90% recall floor sits below what this model achieves, so
  'maximise precision' is free to climb the threshold until it hits the floor.
  Out-of-fold that buys +13.7% precision and +64 alarms,
  at the price of +25 fraud rows let through.
  Priced in rupees the trade is negative by Rs.1,125,170: a missed mule
  transfer costs its full amount, a false alarm costs one Rs.150 review.
  Active policy is 'cost' (set THRESHOLD_POLICY at the top of this
  file to switch). Raising TARGET_RECALL has much the same effect.

==============================================================================
MERCHANT ECONOMICS (test set)
==============================================================================
  A bank pays a fixed sum to review an alert. A merchant declining a good
  customer loses the margin on that order plus the cost of winning them back,
  so the false-positive cost scales with basket size. And a gateway has a
  third action a bank does not: challenge the payment with a step-up.

  Assumptions   chargeback fee Rs.1,250  |  contribution margin 18%  |  goodwill Rs.400
                step-up abandon 8%  |  step-up catch 85%  |  manual review Rs.150
                step-up budget 10% of payments

  Policy                                Total cost   Per txn    Held  Stepped up  Fraud through
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  block/allow at 0.2682             Rs.    185,410      9.27     111           -              1
  accept / step-up / hold           Rs.     99,548      4.98      81         735              0

  Challenge budget used  : 3.67% of 10% (headroom left)
  The budget is not decoration. Minimising cost row by row, a step-up is so
  cheap that the policy will challenge anything carrying more than a fraction
  of a percent of risk - on a book with diffuse scores that reached 94% of
  legitimate traffic in testing. Arithmetically optimal, and it would destroy
  conversion. Friction is a portfolio resource, so it is capped and then spent
  on the payments where a challenge saves the most.

  Adding the challenge action saves Rs.85,862 on 20,000 payments (46% of merchant loss),
  by moving 735 payments off the analyst queue and onto a
  step-up that costs a slice of conversion instead of the whole order. Manual
  holds fall from 111 to 81.

  Card-network dispute covenant (Visa VAMP / Mastercard ECM):
    Expected disputes      : 3.0 of 20,000 payments
    Dispute ratio          : 0.0150%  (ceiling 1.50%)
    Within covenant        : yes
    Note                   : VAMP scores reported fraud plus disputes over
                             settled card-not-present volume, so the ratio above
                             is narrower than the one Visa measures.

  Being straight about this constraint: at 0.5% fraud prevalence the covenant
  is slack by construction - even letting every fraud through would sit under
  the 1.5% ceiling. It starts to bind above 18.8% prevalence at the 92%
  recall the age-ablated model achieves, which is the regime a compromised
  merchant category actually lives in. It is reported because it is the real
  operating limit, not because it is doing work on this dataset.

SPLIT INTEGRITY - do scam incidents straddle train and test?
==============================================================================
  Test fraud rows whose receiver VPA also appears in training fraud: 0/100 (0.0%)
  Recall on 'cold' receivers (100 rows, never seen): 99.00%

  A stratified random split cuts through scam incidents - the Rs.1 probe lands in
  train while its drain lands in test, and one mule VPA is scattered across both.
  The model then recognises a receiver it has already been taught, and the score
  is flattered. Under that split the bleed rate here was 59%.

  Module 1 now emits `ring_id`, so the split groups on the incident and the bleed
  is gone. It cost real headline performance and the numbers above are the ones
  that survived: PR-AUC 0.9955 -> 0.9802, and recall on receivers the model has
  never seen is 99% rather than a flattered 100%. That is the point of measuring
  it - the earlier figure was partly a property of the split, not of the model.

==============================================================================
ABLATION - what is each block of features actually worth?
==============================================================================
  Feature set                           PR-AUC    Recall   Precision        F1
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  everything                            0.9979    0.9900      0.8919    0.9384
  without receiver VPA age              0.9506    0.8600      0.8600    0.8600
  without graph features                0.9675    0.8200      0.9762    0.8913
  without either                        0.8788    0.9200      0.5860    0.7160

  Account age. Every fraudulent receiver in this data is 0-20 days old, so the
  feature will always carry signal here. Module 1 deliberately routes a slice of
  ordinary traffic - and a thin tail of genuine big-ticket payments - to brand
  new VPAs, so age alone is only ~7% precise. Real traffic makes it noisier still,
  and often missing, which is why the row without it is the one to plan against.

  Graph features. Priced against the age-ablated model rather than the full one,
  because the full model is close to saturated and improving it proves nothing.
  With account age removed, adding fan-in and collection velocity moves PR-AUC
  0.8788 -> 0.9506 (+0.0719) - recovering
  60% of the ground lost by dropping account age altogether.

  Read PR-AUC here, not recall. Each row is re-calibrated on its own out-of-fold
  probabilities, so the operating points are not the same point and the recall
  column is not comparable across rows - it moved from 0.92 to 0.86 while precision went the other way,
  which is the threshold moving rather than the model getting worse. PR-AUC is
  threshold-free, which is exactly why it is the column to compare.

  What that buys is a different *kind* of evidence. Account age is an attribute
  of the receiving account, so a fraudster defeats it by ageing a mule for three
  weeks before using it. Fan-in is a property of the ring's behaviour: collecting
  from several victims in minutes is the thing the scam has to do to be the scam.
  A ring cannot age its way out of it.

  And the signal is strictly backward-looking, which the ring assembly shows:
  payment 1 into a mule sees fan-in 1 and looks ordinary, payment 3 sees 2, and
  by payment 5 the third distinct victim has arrived and the hub flag fires. The
  model is not being told the future - it is watching the star form.
